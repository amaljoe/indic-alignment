Okay, let's explain 'reservation' in the Indian context.

**Reservation** in India refers to a system of **affirmative action** or **positive discrimination**. It involves setting aside a certain percentage of seats in educational institutions (especially higher education and professional courses like engineering, medicine) and positions in public sector jobs (like government services and public sector undertakings) for members of historically disadvantaged communities.

The primary goal of reservation is to promote **social justice and equal opportunity** by providing representation and access to education and employment for communities that have faced discrimination and exclusion for centuries, particularly the **Scheduled Castes (SC)**, **Scheduled Tribes (ST)**, and **Other Backward Classes (OBC)**.

**Constitutional Articles:**

Reservation in India derives its basis from specific clauses in the Constitution, primarily:

1.  **Article 15(4):** This article explicitly allows the state to make special provisions for the advancement of any socially and educationally backward classes of citizens, minorities, or other such groups. It is the foundational article empowering the state to implement reservation policies.
2.  **Article 16(4):** This article allows the state to reserve appointments or posts in services under the state for the advancement of any backward class of citizens. This is particularly relevant for public sector employment.

These articles provide the constitutional basis for the reservation system, which is further detailed and implemented through various laws and administrative rules.
